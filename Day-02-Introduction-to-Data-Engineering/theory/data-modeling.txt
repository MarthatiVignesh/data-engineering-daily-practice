DAY 2 - INTRODUCTION TO DATA ENGINEERING
========================================

TOPIC: DATA MODELLING BASICS

1. WHAT IS DATA MODELLING?

Data modelling is the process of designing how data is organized,
stored, and related within a data system.

It helps us understand:

- What data needs to be stored
- How different data elements are related
- What each record represents
- How data should be structured for analysis

2. ENTITY

An entity is a real-world object, concept, or thing about which
we want to store information.

Examples:

- Customer
- Product
- Order
- Employee
- Department

Example:

Customer

Customer_ID
Customer_Name
Email
Phone

3. RELATIONSHIP

A relationship describes how two or more entities are connected.

Example:

Customer ---- places ----> Order

This means a customer can place an order.

Another example:

Order ---- contains ----> Product

This represents the relationship between orders and products.

5. TYPES OF RELATIONSHIPS

A. One-to-One (1:1)
-------------------
One record in one entity is related to one record in another entity.

Example:

Person ---- has ----> Passport

One person has one passport.

B. One-to-Many (1:N)
--------------------
One record can be related to many records.

Example:

Customer ---- places ----> Orders

One customer can place many orders.

C. Many-to-Many (M:N)
----------------------
Many records in one entity can be related to many records
in another entity.

Example:

Students ---- enroll in ----> Courses

One student can enroll in many courses, and one course can
have many students.

6. GRAIN

Grain defines the exact level of detail represented by one row
in a table, especially in a fact table.

Before designing a fact table, we should clearly define its grain.

Example:

Suppose we have an Order Sales table.

If the grain is:

"One row represents one product line in one order."

Then an order containing three different products will produce
three rows.

Example:

Order_ID    Product    Quantity
1001        Laptop       1
1001        Mouse        2
1001        Keyboard     1

The grain is:

One row = one product line in one order.

7. WHY GRAIN IS IMPORTANT

Defining the grain helps us:

- Understand what each row represents
- Avoid incorrect aggregations
- Design fact tables correctly
- Maintain consistent calculations
- Prevent duplicate counting

8. DATA MODELLING EXAMPLE

Business scenario:

An online shopping company wants to analyze sales.

Important entities:

- Customer
- Product
- Order
- Store
- Date

Possible relationships:

Customer ---- places ----> Order
Order ---- contains ----> Product
Order ---- occurs on ----> Date

Possible fact-table grain:

"One row represents one product sold in one order."

9. KEY IDEA

Data modelling defines the structure of data and the relationships
between different data entities.

Entity = What we store information about.

Relationship = How entities are connected.

Grain = What exactly one row represents.

Understanding these three concepts is essential for designing
effective analytical data models.
